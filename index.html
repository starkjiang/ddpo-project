Distributed Direct Preference Optimization

Zhanhong Jiang1
1 Translational AI Center, Iowa State University
Paper (arXiv) Code Slides Video
Abstract
Preference-based reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a compelling framework for aligning policies with human preferences, yet its theoretical foundations in distributed environments remain largely unexplored. While Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) eliminates the need for explicit reward modeling, extending it to federated and decentralized settings introduces fundamental challenges stemming from communication constraints, client drift, and heterogeneous, non-IID preference distributions. Moreover, DPO's trajectory-level log-ratio objective induces nonlinear optimization dynamics and cross-trajectory dependencies that intricately couple learning with network consensus. In this work, we establish the first rigorous convergence and time-complexity theory for distributed DPO. For federated DPO, we characterize convergence behavior through explicit dependence on communication frequency, client heterogeneity, and local drift. For decentralized DPO, we derive guarantees over arbitrary communication topologies, revealing how spectral connectivity fundamentally shapes both consensus formation and optimization efficiency. Beyond standard assumptions, our analysis derives smoothness and variance constants directly from the underlying RL trajectory structure and horizon length. Empirical studies on standard alignment benchmarks further validate our theoretical predictions, demonstrating that the proposed methods achieve both strong theoretical guarantees and scalable practical performance.
